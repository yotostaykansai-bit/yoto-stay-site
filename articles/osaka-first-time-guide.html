First Time in Osaka: 9 Practical Things to Know Before You Go

Cash, trains, etiquette and food — the basics that make a first Osaka trip smoother.

Money

Cards are widely accepted, but small eateries and markets can be cash-first. Carry some yen, and use convenience-store ATMs that accept foreign cards.

Trains

An IC card (tap card) makes local travel simple. Buy or add one early, keep it charged, and screenshot your route before you lose signal underground.

Food etiquette

Queue where locals queue, do not eat while walking in crowded areas, and learn one phrase: 'Gochiso-sama deshita' — thank you for the meal.

Booking a stay

Book through the official listing, read the house rules in full, and keep all communication inside Airbnb. That protects both guest and host.

Prices, timetables and availability change — always confirm on the official booking or operator page before you travel.
